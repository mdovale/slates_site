Color is the map.

Eight slates, eight colors. You remember the yellow one, not a folder name. The color follows the slate everywhere: its header, its card, its numbered square, and the chrome around your text.

Twenty built-in palettes

  • One slate at a time

    View → Slate Appearance… ⌘JCommand-J changes a single slate: its font, size, and line spacing, and its background, header, and accent colors. You see each change as you make it.

  • Or all eight together

    Every palette has a Dark and a Light side, and follows View → Theme. New Palette… and Import File… let you make your own.

  • Reorder by dragging

    Drag a slate by its header to move it. Its color goes with it, so the yellow one is still the yellow one.

  • Hide the ones you are not using

    ⌥⌘1Option-Command-1 through ⌥⌘8Option-Command-8 show or hide a slate. A hidden slate keeps its text until you bring it back.

2Chapter 2

Capture, copy, clear.

Paste a door code, a tracking number, a paragraph. Copy it when you need it. When it has done its job, Copy & Clear puts it on the clipboard and empties the slate.

2 Plain

Slate 2 · Not saved

  • Copy

    The Copy button in every header copies the whole slate. Or select part of it and press ⌘CCommand-C, like anywhere else.

  • Copy & Clear

    In the ⋯ menu of each header. For one-time codes and anything you will paste exactly once.

  • Share

    Send a slate anywhere your Mac can share to, with File → Share.

  • A limit that tells you

    Each slate holds up to 100,000 characters, roughly 15,000 words. Past that, Slates keeps the first 100,000 and says so.

3Chapter 3

Plain, Rich, Code.

Each slate picks its own mode. Plain for codes and lists that must stay exactly as typed. Rich for notes with headings, emphasis, and links. Code for syntax you want colored.

Mode
Appearance

Format → Switch to Plain Text ⇧⌘TShift-Command-T Format → Switch to Rich Text ⇧⌘RShift-Command-R Format → Switch to Code ⇧⌘CShift-Command-C

  • Rich text you write as Markdown

    Type # for a heading and ** for bold. Links stay clickable, and fenced code keeps its colors inside a note.

  • Set like a dictionary

    Each slate can have its own typeface: Palatino for a dictionary entry, Georgia for notes, Menlo for codes.

  • Look Up

    Look up any word the way you do elsewhere on your Mac, including ⌃⌘DControl-Command-D.

6Chapter 6

Your desk, your layout.

Look at all eight, or just one. Stack them in a column or lay them out in a row. Fold the window down to its toolbar when you need the screen back.

List layout
View → Toggle Layout ⌘LCommand-L

  • Collapse to the toolbar

    Window → Collapse Window ⌘MCommand-M folds Slates down to its row of numbered squares. Press ⌘MCommand-M again to bring it back.

  • Focus and list

    ⌘1Command-1 through ⌘8Command-8 focus one slate. ⌘\Command-Backslash shows all of them again.

  • Type the way you like

    Word wrap, tab size, indentation, and a dozen syntax themes live in Settings → Editor. Fonts, text size, and line spacing can be set per slate or for all of them.

7Chapter 7

Menu bar or Dock. Synced or not. Backed up.

Slates can live in the Dock, the menu bar, or both. Your text is stored locally by default; optional iCloud sync keeps your Macs in step. Backups happen on their own.

  • Dock, menu bar, or both

    Settings → General → Activate With: a standard Dock app, a menu bar icon that opens a compact window, or both.

  • iCloud, only if you ask

    Sync is off until you choose Turn On… in Settings → Data. Your text travels through Apple’s iCloud, not through us.

  • Automatic snapshots

    At most once an hour while you are changing things, and again when you quit. Slates keeps the latest 40, for up to 90 days.

  • Backups you can hold

    File → Save Backup… ⌥⌘SOption-Command-S writes a file. File → Restore Backup… ⇧⌘OShift-Command-O brings one back, after asking.

8Chapter 8

Private by design.

  • No analytics.
  • No account.
  • No Slates server.
  • Stored locally by default; optional iCloud sync.

Slates has no usage tracking, no crash-reporting service, and no ads. There is nothing to sign in to. This page follows the same rule: no trackers, no cookies, and the demos above forget everything when you leave.
