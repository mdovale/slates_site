# slate
**/sleɪt/**
_noun_ · _verb_ · occasionally _adjective_
---
## noun
**slate** (_plural_ **slates**)
**1.** _Geology_. A fine-grained, foliated metamorphic rock, typically blue-gray, that splits readily into thin, even plates. It is shale after pressure and time: the same clay, reorganized so that it prefers a plane. Quarried for roofing, for flooring, and for the small tablets on which children once learned their letters and then wiped them away.
**2.** A tablet of this rock, or a board made in imitation of it, used for writing. The useful property is not permanence. It is that you can fill it, read it, and clear it. Hence **a clean slate**: a surface restored, not a history rewritten.
**3.** A thin plate of rock used especially to roof a building. _The chapel still wears its original slates, darker after rain._
**4.** A list of candidates, nominees, or proposed measures, offered as a group rather than as eight separate arguments. _The committee advanced a slate of four._ In this sense the word is American in flavor, and slightly official even when the list was hashed out in a hallway.
**5.** _Informal, computing_. A single colored scratchpad kept in view among a small, fixed set. Not a file. Not a notebook. A pad you return to because you remember the color, the way you remember which mug is yours.
---
## verb
**slate** (_slated_, _slating_, _slates_)
**1.** _transitive_. To cover with slate. _They slated the roof before the rains._
**2.** To designate, appoint, or schedule for a particular time or purpose. _The launch is slated for Thursday._ _She is slated to present in the 4 p.m. slot, which means 4:10._ This is the office verb: dry, a little bureaucratic, hard to replace. You do not “pencil in” a GA.
**3.** _Chiefly British, transitive_. To criticize severely. _The notices slated the revival._ Rare in California except when someone is being funny on purpose.
---
## adjective
**slate** (_slate-gray_, _slaty_)
Of the color of the rock: a cool gray with a blue in it, the color of the Bay when the fog has not yet decided. _A slate sky over the lot at Building 18._ Related: **slate blue**, which is trying too hard, and **slaty**, which the geologists prefer.
---
## etymology
Middle English _slate_, _sclate_, from Old French _esclate_, _esclat_ “splinter, piece split off” (compare modern French _éclat_: a fragment, and also a burst of brilliance, which is a lot to ask of a roofing tile). The geological sense is the oldest in English. The writing-tablet sense follows from the stone’s habit of cleaving into something you can hold in one hand. The political “list” and the scheduling verb come later. **Blank slate** is a calque of Latin _tabula rasa_; **clean slate** is the more domestic cousin, closer to the classroom than to Locke.
---
## usage
Prefer **slated for** when a date or occasion is attached (_slated for Thursday_) and **slated to** when a person or an act follows (_slated to ship_). **Clean slate** is the idiom for a fresh start you can actually perform — wipe the pad. **Blank slate** is the philosophical metaphor, and a little grand for a door code.
Do not confuse with _slat_, a thin strip of wood. A slat is what a crate is made of. A slate is what you write on, or what you are, or what the sky is doing.
In this building one still hears _put it on the slate_ — meaning keep it on the small pad, not in the document that will outlive the week. The other sense, the restaurant sense (a running account, a tab), is not dead. It just migrated to software.
